# Closed Loop - Vision & Future Ideas

"The purpose of Closed Loop is not to provide all the answers. It is to help people ask better questions."

# Vision Statement

The world's scientific and engineering knowledge is growing faster than any individual can absorb.

Closed Loop exists to make that knowledge understandable, interactive and collaborative.

By combining simulation, real-world research, artificial intelligence and community participation, Closed Loop aims to become the bridge between scientific discovery and practical understanding.

Instead of asking people to read hundreds of research papers, Closed Loop allows them to explore technologies, ask questions, build complete systems and understand why they work.

The goal is not to replace scientists, engineers or educators.

The goal is to amplify their impact by making knowledge more accessible, transparent and useful for everyone.

## Vision

Closed Loop is not intended to become just another city-building game.

Its purpose is to become an interactive research platform where anyone can explore, design and understand real-world circular economy systems.

Users should be able to experiment with waste processing, renewable energy, water treatment, food production, AI infrastructure and industrial ecosystems while learning the real science behind each technology.

The simulator should encourage curiosity.

Every decision should lead users to ask:

> "Why does this work?"

and provide the tools to discover the answer.

Annual Utilisation Analysis

One of the key differences between Closed Loop and traditional factory simulators is that the focus is annual infrastructure planning rather than real-time production.

Machines do not simply operate as "On" or "Off".

Instead, every machine has an Operating Level that represents the percentage of its annual design capacity being utilised.

# National Infrastructure Planning

The Closed Loop simulator is designed around modular infrastructure hubs, with each hub representing approximately **500,000 people**.

Rather than creating one fixed national design, the simulator allows AI and the user to optimise every hub independently based on regional characteristics.

The objective is to determine the most effective combination of technologies for each location while maximising overall national performance.

## AI Engineering Team

Closed Loop's `AI Engineering Team` is a proposed multi-agent architecture that treats specialised AI agents as members of an engineering organisation. The goal is to provide expert, traceable, and modular reasoning about technologies, resources and system integration rather than a single all-knowing chatbot.

### Overview

- Specialist agents focus on one technology or domain (for example: Anaerobic Digestion, Gasification, Hydrogen, Batteries, Data Centres).
- Resource agents focus on resource types and flows (for example: Organic Waste, Electricity, Water, Heat, CO₂).
- Bridge agents validate interfaces between technologies and resources (for example: does digester output match engine input? Are pressures and temperatures compatible?).
- A Systems Engineer agent coordinates answers from specialists and bridges, producing integrated recommendations and highlighting conflicts.

### Structure and Roles

- Chief Systems/Integration Agent: orchestrates queries, aggregates specialist outputs and produces system-level synthesis.
- Technology Specialists: become deep experts on one technology, tracking literature, operational facilities, limitations, and typical performance ranges.
- Resource Specialists: track supply characteristics, variability, contaminants, transport and handling constraints.
- Bridge Agents: validate and quantify interfaces between technology and resource nodes, compute flow compatibility and flag mismatches.

### Behaviour and Workflow

- Agents update independently when new evidence arrives (papers, datasets, facility reports). Only the relevant specialists re-evaluate their conclusions.
- When assessing a system, the Systems Engineer queries the relevant specialists and bridge agents, then combines their answers into a single recommendation with provenance.
- Specialists may disagree; the architecture supports explicit challenge and revision cycles (similar to engineering design reviews) so that conflicting assumptions are exposed and resolved.

### Traceability and Explainability

- Every recommendation includes a provenance trail: which agents contributed, what evidence they used, key assumptions, and confidence levels.
- Users can inspect contributing agents and drill into evidence (papers, sources, datasets) and assumptions used in a recommendation.

### Confidence Scores and Uncertainty

- Agents provide confidence scores for outputs and for each connection in a chain (for example: confidence that a pathway from waste to algae production is technically viable).
- Confidence categories are exposed in the UI (Well understood / Promising / Limited evidence / Research gap) and drive suggested research or pilot actions.

### Research Map & Visualisation

- The system can present an overlay or network view showing connections between technologies and resources annotated with confidence and evidence density.
- Colour-coded nodes/links indicate maturity and research gaps, helping researchers and practitioners prioritise follow-up work.

### Incremental Development Strategy

- The architecture is designed to scale: start with a small set of core specialists (for example: Waste, Energy, Water, Systems) and grow organically as the project matures.
- Early milestones focus on proving the pattern: independent agent updates, provenance capture, simple bridge validations, and a basic Systems Engineer synthesis.

### Use Cases and Value

- Automated bottleneck detection for community designs (identify weakest link and suggest targeted research topics).
- Transparent recommendations where users can trace the evidence and assumptions behind a decision.
- A continuously improving Research Hub that benefits from selective updates when real-world evidence changes.

### Principle: Assist, Do Not Replace

- The AI Engineering Team is explicitly positioned as a decision support system. Human experts remain responsible for validation and for high-consequence decisions. The system highlights trade-offs, supporting evidence and alternative opinions rather than asserting unquestionable truth.

## Why Multiple AI Agents?

Closed Loop deliberately uses a team of specialised AI agents rather than a single general-purpose assistant.

Real engineering projects are rarely designed by one expert. They are developed through collaboration between specialists in different disciplines who challenge assumptions, review each other's work and combine their expertise into an integrated solution.

The AI Engineering Team mirrors this process. Each specialist is responsible for becoming deeply knowledgeable in a specific technology or engineering domain. Bridge agents validate the interactions between technologies. The Systems Engineer combines their findings into a coherent design while preserving the evidence and assumptions behind every recommendation.

This approach provides:

- Better transparency
- Better traceability
- Easier maintenance
- Independent updates to individual domain knowledge
- Reduced hallucination risk through cross-validation
- Explainable engineering decisions

The objective is not to replace engineers. The objective is to provide an AI-assisted engineering team that accelerates exploration while keeping humans responsible for validation and final decisions.

A practical first-stage research question for grant proposals is:

Demonstrating measurable improvement in design exploration, bottleneck identification and traceable recommendations provides evidence to scale the architecture and justify further investment.

# Collaborative Research Profiles

Closed Loop should support multiple technology profiles rather than a single fixed database.

The simulator should allow users to switch between different engineering datasets while keeping the same plant layout.

Examples:

- Verified Technologies
- CSIRO
- University of Melbourne
- MIT
- Fraunhofer Institute
- My Research
- Project-Specific Research
- Future Closed Loop

Changing the active profile automatically recalculates the entire simulator using that profile's machine efficiencies, resource outputs, economics, and operating assumptions.

The plant remains identical.

Only the engineering knowledge changes.

---

# Technology Branches

Technology profiles should behave similarly to software version control.

The official **Verified** database remains unchanged.

Researchers and organisations may create independent branches containing modified machine data.

Examples include:

- Improved efficiencies
- Alternative chemical pathways
- Different operating temperatures
- New catalysts
- Lower energy consumption
- Additional resource outputs

Branches never overwrite the official database.

Instead, users choose which branch they wish to simulate.

---

# Research Sandbox

Every user should be able to create their own experimental technology branch.

This allows researchers, universities, companies and hobbyists to safely explore new ideas without affecting the verified knowledge base.

Examples:

- New machine concepts
- Experimental outputs
- Modified efficiencies
- Prototype technologies
- Alternative resource pathways

Projects created with custom technologies remain compatible while clearly identifying which technologies have been modified.

- Universities
- Government research organisations
