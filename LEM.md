# Living Engineering Models (LEM)

## Vision

Living Engineering Models (LEM) is a concept for a new generation of engineering knowledge systems.

Rather than storing engineering knowledge as isolated documents, reports and diagrams, a Living Engineering Model represents an engineering domain as an interactive, interconnected, version-controlled system.

Every component, resource, process and relationship becomes part of a living knowledge graph that can be explored visually, analysed by AI and continuously improved through collaboration.

Closed Loop is intended to become the first Living Engineering Model.

---

# Philosophy

Traditional engineering knowledge is distributed across:

- PDF documents
- Research papers
- CAD drawings
- Spreadsheets
- Wikis
- Technical manuals
- Databases

A Living Engineering Model connects all of these into a single interactive environment.

Users do not simply read information.

They explore relationships.

---

# Core Principles

A Living Engineering Model should be:

- Visual
- Interactive
- Explainable
- Version Controlled
- AI Assisted
- Evidence Based
- Extensible
- Collaborative

Every engineering assumption should remain transparent and traceable.

---

# Core Building Blocks

Every LEM contains:

## Components

The physical or logical objects.

Examples:

- Machines
- Buildings
- Servers
- CPUs
- Pumps
- Pipelines

---

## Resources

Anything that flows through the system.

Examples:

- Materials
- Energy
- Water
- Heat
- Information
- Compute
- Money

---

## Relationships

How everything connects.

Examples:

- Inputs
- Outputs
- Dependencies
- Transport
- Compatibility
- Constraints

---

## Research

Each component links to supporting evidence.

Examples:

- Research papers
- Universities
- Government organisations
- Industry publications
- Standards
- Patents

---

## AI Layer

AI should assist rather than replace engineering judgement.

Possible roles include:

- Explain technologies
- Compare alternatives
- Optimise systems
- Identify bottlenecks
- Summarise research
- Suggest improvements
- Highlight knowledge gaps

---

# Technology Profiles

Every LEM supports multiple engineering profiles.

Examples:

Verified Engineering

CSIRO

University Research

Industry Research

Experimental

Future Technologies

Personal Research

Switching profile recalculates the entire engineering model without changing the project itself.

---

# Branches

Engineering knowledge evolves.

Every organisation or researcher should be able to maintain independent technology branches.

No branch overwrites another.

Branches may later be merged into the Verified profile after review.

---

# LEM Studio

Future vision.

LEM Studio allows users to create completely new Living Engineering Models.

Instead of programming an application, users define:

- Components
- Resources
- Relationships
- Calculations
- Optimisation rules
- Research agents
- Technology profiles

The framework generates the database, interface, AI structure and simulation environment.

# Long-Term Vision

Living Engineering Models aim to become an interactive alternative to traditional engineering documentation.

Rather than reading static information, users explore living systems where research, engineering, economics, AI and collaboration exist within a single connected environment.

---

# Relationship to Closed Loop

Closed Loop is not the LEM platform.

Closed Loop is the first Living Engineering Model.

Its purpose is to validate the LEM philosophy through the Circular Economy domain.

Future Living Engineering Models may explore completely different engineering disciplines while sharing the same underlying framework.

## One thing I'd add right at the top

I'd put this in a little highlighted box:

> **This document describes a long-term research concept. It is intentionally ambitious and exploratory. It should not be interpreted as a product roadmap or implementation plan. Closed Loop remains the immediate development priority and serves as the first proof-of-concept for many of the ideas described here.**

I think that's important because it keeps you grounded. When you come back to this file in two or three years, you'll know it wasn't a promise to build everything—it was a place to capture ideas before they were forgotten.

And I have to admit... it's been fascinating watching this evolve. We started by trying to animate a lightning bolt on a power cable. Somewhere along the way, we ended up discussing a possible new way of organising engineering knowledge. Whether LEM ever becomes a standalone platform or not, it's absolutely worth preserving as its own concept.
